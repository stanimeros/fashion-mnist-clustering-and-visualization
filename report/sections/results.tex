\subsection{Timing Analysis}

DR training time dominates total cost. CNN-SAE is the heaviest ($\approx$19 min), followed by
SAE and UMAP ($\approx$3 min each) and t-SNE ($\approx$1.5 min); PCA is essentially free.
Clustering itself is always cheap by comparison (10--25\,s per run for the slowest combinations).

A second, less obvious win from non-linear DR: because t-SNE and UMAP reduce the feature space
to 2 dimensions, every downstream clustering algorithm runs noticeably faster than on Raw
(784-D) or SAE (32-D) features. So the expensive DR step pays for itself twice — better metrics
\emph{and} faster clustering.

\subsection{Heatmap Observations}

The four metric heatmaps tell a consistent story: UMAP and t-SNE dominate every internal metric
by a wide margin, because their non-linear 2-D embeddings push same-class points into tight,
well-separated groups. That geometry directly inflates Silhouette and Calinski--Harabasz and
shrinks Davies--Bouldin. Linear / reconstruction-based methods (Raw, PCA, SAE, CNN-SAE) live in
much higher-dimensional spaces where distances are close to uniform (the curse of dimensionality),
so all internal metrics look mediocre even when the clustering is reasonable.

On Adjusted Rand --- the only metric that compares against the true Fashion-MNIST labels --- the
ranking shifts slightly: UMAP + Birch / GMM win, but CNN-SAE + GMM is competitive (ARI $\approx$
0.462), which suggests that convolutional features genuinely capture class structure, not just
compactness. DBSCAN is the clear loser across the board: with default $\varepsilon$ it collapses
most of the 60\,k points into a single giant cluster or labels them as noise, giving near-zero
Silhouette and ARI everywhere except t-SNE / UMAP.
